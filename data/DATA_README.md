All data in this dataset is simulated. It was generated by a Python script written by Claude Opus 5.5 (AI). The data is cleaner than real app data would be: it has no missed scans, wrong dates or unmarked items.

# Simulated fridge usage history

## Dataset

**Purpose.** Training and testing a scikit-learn classifier for a fridge management app that predicts whether a fridge item will be eaten or wasted.

**Simulated household.** One adult living alone in Finland. Works on weekdays and eats lunch at work, cooks favourite dishes at home a few times a week, snacks during the day and sometimes late in the evening, and keeps leftovers in the fridge.

**Period.** 2025-09-01 to 2026-08-31 (12 months), simulated day by day.

**Files.**

- `fridge_history.json`: one record per fridge item, from the moment it is added until it is removed (JSON array).
- `favorite_dishes.json`: the favourite dishes (JSON array).
- `products_catalogue.json`: the product catalogue with shelf lives (JSON array), as a real app would have in its product database.
- `DATA_README.md`: this file, written by `generate_fridge_data.py` together with the data.

### Fields of fridge_history.json

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| id | int | Unique item id, 1, 2, ... in the order the items were added. |
| name | str | Product name, e.g. "Cucumber". Leftovers are named "<dish name> (leftovers)". |
| category | str | dairy, eggs, meat, fish, bread, vegetable, fruit, ready_meal, condiment or leftovers. |
| barcode | str | EAN-13 code. Real code from `products.json` if the product is listed there, otherwise a fake store-internal code (prefix 20-29, valid check digit). Every product has one code; the leftovers of each dish have their own fake code. |
| added_date | str (YYYY-MM-DD) | Day the item was put in the fridge (bought, or cooked for leftovers). |
| expiry_date | str (YYYY-MM-DD) | Date on the package; for leftovers the cooking day + 3 days. |
| opened_date | str (YYYY-MM-DD) or null | Day the first portion was used. null if the item was never opened. |
| removed_date | str (YYYY-MM-DD) | Day the item left the fridge: last portion eaten, or thrown away. |
| outcome | str | "eaten" (all portions eaten) or "wasted" (thrown away, even if partly eaten). |
| consumed_by | str or null | How the last portion was eaten: "dish" = used in cooking a favourite dish, or leftovers of a dish eaten later; "snack" = eaten without cooking (breakfast, sandwich, snack, late-evening snack, ready meal); null if wasted. |
| dish_id | int or null | Id of the favourite dish the item was used in (for leftovers: the dish they came from). null for snacks and wasted items. |

What a real app could know, and may be used as model features:

- When an item is added: name, category, barcode, added_date, expiry_date, and the product's shelf lives from `products_catalogue.json`.
- On a later day: whether the item has been opened (opened_date before that day), which items are in the fridge that day, and the outcomes of items removed before that day (e.g. each product's past waste rate).
- Not as features: removed_date, outcome, consumed_by and dish_id of the item itself, which are only known when it leaves the fridge and reveal the label.
- Not in the data at all: the personal habits (where products are kept, blind spots, habit purchases; see Simulation assumptions). An app cannot know them directly; it can only learn their effect from the history, e.g. as a product's past waste rate. No new fields were added to fridge_history.json for them.

## Simulation assumptions

All values below are the constants at the top of `generate_fridge_data.py`. Probabilities are per occasion unless stated otherwise.

### Daily routine

- The simulation starts with an empty fridge and a big shop on the first day.
- A day at home, in order: spoiled items that have been noticed are thrown away; items that are overlooked today are chosen (see Waste tendencies); coffee with milk (85 %, one portion of milk); breakfast (90 %); lunch; afternoon snack (60 %); shopping; dinner; late-evening snack.
- Lunch: Monday-Friday at work, with leftovers taken in a lunch box (see Leftovers). Saturday-Sunday at home: leftovers, otherwise a snack.
- Dinner on a normal day: first the dish the person would cook is chosen (see Cooking). If its key ingredient is at most 2 days from its last good day, leftovers are skipped and the person cooks with at least 85 %. Otherwise leftovers come first if there are some and they appeal (see Leftovers), then cooking with the weekday probability below. If no dish can be made, the person shops for one dish with 40 %. Not cooking: eating out with the weekday probability below, otherwise a snack dinner.
- Late-evening snack: 35 % on Friday and Saturday evenings, 20 % on other evenings.
- At the end of every day (also when away) items at their last good day become spoiled.

| Weekday | Cook | Eat out |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | 65 % | 10 % |
| Tuesday | 65 % | 10 % |
| Wednesday | 60 % | 10 % |
| Thursday | 60 % | 15 % |
| Friday | 35 % | 40 % |
| Saturday | 60 % | 30 % |
| Sunday | 70 % | 10 % |

### Shopping

- One bigger weekly shop; its day is chosen every Monday: Thursday 5 %, Friday 50 %, Saturday 30 %, Sunday 15 %. Skipped if the person is away that day.
- Before the weekly shop the person checks the fridge with 80 %. Otherwise each item at home is remembered with 75 %, so products already at home are bought again. Overlooked and spoiled items are never counted.
- Planned dishes: 2-3 favourite dishes for the coming week, chosen by preference (see Cooking), with different key ingredients and the key ingredient in season. Missing portions of their ingredients are bought as whole packages; out-of-season ingredients are skipped.
- Restocking: every product in season with a restock probability above 0 is bought (one package) with that probability when no package of it with at least 50 % of the portions left is known to be at home. Habit products (Yogurt, Lettuce, Grapes) are bought with their restock probability at every weekly shop, even when some is at home.
- Trips: if a trip starts within 7 days, the person remembers it at the weekly shop with 70 %; a holiday of at least 7 days is always remembered. Then at most one dish per 2 days before the trip is planned, and nothing is restocked if the trip starts within 3 days. There are no top-up trips in those days either.
- Top-up trips on other days: when an essential is not found in the fridge (no Milk -> buy Milk, no bread -> buy Rye bread), the person goes to the shop with 50 %. Shopping for one dish: see Daily routine.
- Special offers: at a weekly shop, with 15 % a random regularly bought product is on offer and 1-2 extra packages are bought.
- Shopping while hungry: on any trip, with 15 % 1-2 impulse items are bought from: Karelian pies, Cold-smoked salmon, Cream cheese, Grapes, Quark, Toast bread, Strawberries, Grill sausages (if in season).
- Red-label discounts: on any trip, with 10 % one discounted item with only 1-2 days left is bought from: Minced meat, Chicken strips, Salmon fillet, Sliced ham, Rye bread, Karelian pies, Milk.

### Snacking

Eating without cooking follows templates. A template is possible when its first part is in the fridge; each used part takes one portion of one item. A template's weight is multiplied by 3.0 if an item for its first part is opened and by 5.0 if one is at most 2 days from its last good day, and by the highest appeal of the foods for its first part (see Personal habits). Within a part, the product is chosen with the same factors (base weight 1).

- sandwich: bread (always), Butter (80 %), one of Cheese / Cream cheese (80 %), one of Sliced ham / Cold-smoked salmon (70 %), one of Cucumber / Tomatoes / Bell pepper / Lettuce (80 %)
- porridge: Milk (always), one of Lingonberry jam / Strawberries (60 %)
- yogurt bowl: one of Yogurt / Quark (always), one of Strawberries / Grapes / Lingonberry jam (40 %)
- eggs: Eggs (always), bread (70 %), Butter (50 %)
- karelian pies: Karelian pies (always), Butter (60 %), Eggs (30 %)
- fruit: fruit (always)
- raw vegetables: one of Carrots / Cucumber / Bell pepper / Tomatoes (always)
- ready meal: Liver casserole (always), Lingonberry jam (50 %)
- sausage: one of Ring sausage / Grill sausages (always), bread (40 %)
- festive plate: one of Christmas ham / Rosolli / Carrot casserole / Pickled herring (always), one of Christmas ham / Rosolli / Carrot casserole / Pickled herring (70 %), one of Potatoes / New potatoes (50 %)
- leftovers: leftovers (always)

- Breakfast: sandwich 5, porridge 2, yogurt bowl 3, eggs 1, karelian pies 1
- Weekend lunch: sandwich 3, eggs 2, karelian pies 1, ready meal 2, sausage 1, festive plate 4
- Afternoon snack: fruit 7, raw vegetables 3, sandwich 3, yogurt bowl 3, karelian pies 2
- Snack dinner: ready meal 4, sandwich 3, eggs 2, sausage 3, festive plate 6
- Late-evening snack: sandwich 4, leftovers 3, yogurt bowl 2, sausage 1, fruit 3, festive plate 3

### Leftovers

- After cooking, one serving is eaten and servings - 1 (+- 1) portions go into one leftovers box (category leftovers). The box can be eaten from the next day and spoils after 3 days.
- Base probability of eating one portion when leftovers are found: weekday lunch box 80 %, weekend lunch 70 %, dinner 70 %. It is multiplied by rating / 4 and by 0.85 for every day after the first. Leftovers are also a late-evening snack template.
- Eaten leftovers have consumed_by "dish" and the id of their dish.

### Seasonal effects

- Summer (Jun, Jul, Aug): cooking probability x0.8, eating out x1.3; dishes with season "summer" are favoured.
- Winter (Oct, Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar): dishes with season "winter" (soups, casseroles) are favoured.
- Seasonal products, bought only when sold: Grill sausages (1 May-31 Aug), Christmas ham (1 Dec-31 Dec), Pickled herring (1 Jun-30 Jun, 1 Dec-31 Dec), New potatoes (10 Jun-31 Aug), Strawberries (15 Jun-15 Aug), Rosolli (1 Dec-31 Dec), Carrot casserole (1 Dec-31 Dec).
- Christmas: Christmas foods (Christmas ham, Rosolli, Carrot casserole, Pickled herring) are eaten as a "festive plate". Between 10 Dec and 31 Dec each restocked Christmas food gets an extra package with 50 % and the cooking probability is x0.6.

### Personal habits

Stable habits of this person, the same all year. They are not in the output files: an app could only learn their effect from the history.

- Where products are kept (column Place in the product table; leftovers boxes: back). Food in sight gets used, food at the back of a shelf or in the crisper drawer is forgotten. Factor on the daily probability of overlooking an item: door x0.5, shelf x1.0, counter x1.0, drawer x1.6, back x2.0.
- Personal blind spots, factor on the daily probability of overlooking the product: Milk x0.0, Butter x0.0, Rye bread x0.0, Leek x2.0, Cream cheese x2.0, Sour cream x2.0. Daily staples are never overlooked; products bought for one dish are often forgotten after it.
- Habit purchases: bought out of routine or good intentions at every weekly shop with their restock probability, even when some is at home, but rarely chosen when something tastier is available. Appeal when choosing what to eat (1 = normal): Yogurt 0.5, Lettuce 0.5, Grapes 0.5.

### Waste tendencies

- An item is wasted only when it is not eaten by its last good day (expiry date, or end of the opened shelf life if earlier).
- Each morning an item is overlooked for the day with probability = category base (table below) x place factor x personal factor (see Personal habits), x0.5 for opened items. Overlooked items are not eaten and not counted when shopping, which also leads to buying duplicates.
- Taking an item: first the product (see Snacking), then among packages of it an opened one with 85 %, then the one closest to its last good day with 70 %, otherwise a random one. Similar items share the same meals, so with more of them in the fridge each one is more likely to spoil.
- Spoiled items are thrown away the day after their last good day, or with 35 % noticed late and thrown away 2-6 days after it.

| dairy | 5 % |
| bread | 5 % |

### Noise

- Remaining shelf life at purchase: +- 15 % of the shelf life (rounded, at least 1 day).
- Opened shelf life: +- 1 day(s), at least 1 day.
- Leftover portions: +- 1.
- All daily decisions (meals, snacks, shopping, dish choice, overlooking, noticing) are random draws with the probabilities above, so the patterns are not deterministic.

### Target waste rate

The parameters were tuned for an overall waste rate of 12 %-20 % of items; the generated data has 18.1 %. Reasoning:

- Finnish households throw away about 23 kg of avoidable food per person per year, about 4-5 % of the food they buy by weight; the largest groups are vegetables, home-cooked food (leftovers) and milk products (Silvennoinen et al. 2014, British Food Journal; Silvennoinen 2020, Luke). The main reasons are mould and passed best-before or use-by dates.
- One-person households waste more food per person than larger households.
- The per-item rate here is higher than the share by weight, because only perishable fridge items are included (no pantry or frozen food) and an item counts as wasted even when only its last part is thrown away. Roughly one fridge item in five to eight ending (at least partly) in the bin is therefore plausible.

## Limitations

- One simulated person with fixed habits; the parameters are assumptions informed by research, not fitted to real data. Real habits change over time (e.g. after noticing repeated waste).
- The outcome is per item (eaten or wasted), not the amount wasted; a portion is an abstract use, not a weight.
- Not simulated: freezing, taking food along on trips, guests, sharing food, illness, pantry and frozen food, and the fridge app itself (reminders would change behaviour).
- Spoilage follows the dates exactly. Real people often eat food after the best-before date, or throw it away before it, based on smell and look.
- Shelf lives are fixed per product with simple noise; temperature, brands and package sizes are not modelled.
- Shops, prices and offers are simplified; public holidays are only included through the listed trips.
- Real app data would contain missed scans, wrong dates and items removed without a record; this data is complete and clean.
- Items still in the fridge on the last day are left out (13 items), which slightly under-represents long-lasting items added near the end.

## Regenerating the data

Random seed: 42. Run `python generate_fridge_data.py` (Python 3.7+, standard library only) in the folder where the files should be written. The same seed and constants always give identical files. Change RANDOM_SEED for another sample with the same behaviour. If `products.json` is present, only the barcodes change.
